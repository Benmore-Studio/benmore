How one timeout sweep picks rows. Every minute, per state with a `timeout:`, the checker reads the rows past their deadline oldest first (ordered by updated_at, then id) and tries at most 500 of them. A row still waiting out a failed attempt (timeout_effects: 1m, 2m, 4m ... up to an hour) is passed over and does not count against the 500, so rows whose timeout keeps failing never hold back the rows behind them. A sweep reads at most 10,000 rows of a state. When it stops at either number, the next sweep continues after the last row it read, and once the rows run out the following sweep starts from the oldest again, so a larger backlog only delays the rows behind it (about one sweep per 10,000 waiting rows). After a restart the first sweep starts from the oldest row. Failures are logged once per sweep and state: `WORKFLOW TIMEOUT ERROR [<workflow>] state=<state>: <n> row(s) could not time out (first id=<id>: <error>); each is retried on its own doubling wait`. At most 50,000 failure waits are kept per process; past that one is dropped and its row is tried again sooner.
