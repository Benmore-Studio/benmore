Exercise a hosted connector from a script (stdlib only; any HTTP client works). Origin is the app instance, for example https://<app>-dev.<platform domain>; resource is <origin>/api/_mcp. (1) Sign in a test app user with a cookie jar: GET /api/_csrf, then POST /login {email, password} with X-CSRF-Token and Accept: application/json. (2) Register a client: POST /oauth/register {client_name, redirect_uris: ["http://127.0.0.1:<port>/cb"], grant_types: [authorization_code, refresh_token], response_types: [code], token_endpoint_auth_method: none, scope: "<tool permissions>"}; loopback http redirects are accepted, and scopes the app does not offer are dropped; registration is limited to 20 per hour per client address (an IPv6 client by its /64; 429 with Retry-After past it), a 64 KiB body, a client_name of at most 256 bytes and at most 16 redirect URIs of at most 8192 bytes each. (3) Make a PKCE S256 pair (verifier 43-128 chars, challenge = base64url(sha256(verifier)) without padding) and GET /oauth/authorize?response_type=code&client_id&redirect_uri&state&scope&code_challenge&code_challenge_method=S256&resource=<resource> with the cookie and without following redirects; an app with several workspaces first answers a picker (repeat with &workspace=<id>). (4) The consent page's form posts to /oauth/authorize: send back every hidden input unchanged (response_type, client_id, redirect_uri, state, scope, code_challenge, code_challenge_method, resource, workspace, connector_nonce, consent) plus decision=approve (deny refuses; auto_approve=1 only when offered and wanted), form-encoded with Origin: <origin>. The 302 Location carries code and state; the browser never needs to reach the loopback port. (5) POST /oauth/token form {grant_type: authorization_code, code, redirect_uri, client_id, code_verifier, resource}; refresh with {grant_type: refresh_token, refresh_token, client_id, resource}. (6) POST JSON-RPC to <resource> with Authorization: Bearer <access_token>, Accept: application/json, text/event-stream and MCP-Protocol-Version; an SSE answer carries the JSON in its last data: line. tools/call of a command tool returns operation_id and review_url. (7) Approve as the signed-in person: GET review_url with the cookie, read the hidden _csrf input (bound to that session and operation) and POST it form-encoded to the form action (<review_url>/approve) with Origin: <origin>; a 303 back to the review page means approved. (8) Call _commit {operation_id}, then _status. Use dedicated test accounts and keep their passwords out of scripts and logs; approval needs a direct cookie session, never the bearer token.
