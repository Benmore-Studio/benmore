Events and rooms stay within one app; rooms and events of tables carrying the tenant key stay within one tenant. Established streams recheck credentials, read scopes and room membership (cached for at most 5 seconds and rechecked on every heartbeat), so a revoked session stops receiving events within 5 seconds and is disconnected at the next heartbeat; an anonymous stream likewise ends once the app stops admitting anonymous realtime (features.ws_anonymous turned off or auth added, at the reload). Who gets the full `{table, action}` event follows the table's read mode: anon/everyone tables reach every viewer; member-of, owner_or_role, declared self and owner (user_id) tables give the writer and admins the full event and every other reader the generic `{table:'', action:'refresh'}`, even inside one tenant; tenant tables (those carrying the `groups:` key) stay in the writer's tenant. A table without the tenant key (an app without `groups:`, a `groups.shared` catalog, any table with no tenant column) is not confined to a tenant: with no row scope (no user_id, not member-of/owner_or_role) every reader of the table gets the full event, otherwise the rules above apply in every tenant, so a reader who sees an owner row through a record share (`/share`) gets the generic refresh. Anyone who cannot read the table hears nothing. Hook `sql:` writes are scoped like flow writes: a hook job by the triggering row's tenant key and user_id, a SQL-only update hook by the writing session (api({at:'hooks'}) → sql_refresh). Events are delivered in order, without holding up the request that caused them. Server room sends in grouped apps require a tenant from the session or triggering row; background sends without that context fail.
