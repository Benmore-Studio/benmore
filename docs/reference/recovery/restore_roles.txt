A database restore (restore_db, benmore restore, boot auto-recovery) rolls data back and keeps the live sign-in state (api(at:"recovery.database_restore")); in the same transaction it applies the live role state to the restored copy. A restore does not bring back a role that was taken away: afterwards no account holds a role it does not hold in the live database. For an account in both, the primary role (`_benmore_users.role`) is the lower of the snapshot's and the live one: `admin` ranks above every role and `user` (the role of a new account) below every role, so an admin demoted since the snapshot stays demoted and a role given since the snapshot is not kept; between two other role names the live one stands. A role grant (`_benmore_user_roles`, global or for one group) survives only when the live database still holds the same grant (same account, role and group) and it has not expired there, and it takes the earlier of the two expiry times; a grant made since the snapshot is not added. An account that is only in the snapshot comes back with role `user` and no role grants when the live database once held it (deleted since): its id is at or below the highest account id the live database has handed out, or a live `_benmore_audit_log` row is about it (table_name `_benmore_users`, row_id the account id; a deletion is such a row). An erased account keeps its row and counts as an account in both. An account only in the snapshot that the live database never held keeps the snapshot's primary role and role grants: restoring a backup onto a recreated or new database brings every role assignment back, except for backup accounts whose ids the new database has already handed out to accounts of its own (create no accounts before such a restore, or give those roles again). The app is never left without an admin who can sign in, counted the way the last-admin guard counts (an account that is neither deactivated nor held at a temporary password, has a verified email when the app sets `auth.require_verified`, and is admin by its primary role or by a global admin grant with no expiry): when these rules would leave none, the accounts that are such admins now keep that standing (`admins_kept: "live"`), and when none of them exists in the snapshot, the snapshot's own admin roles and global admin grants are kept, including ones removed since (`admins_kept: "snapshot"`). Either case puts a `warning` in the answer: review who is an admin after such a restore. `auth.require_verified` is read from the app.yaml of the instance being restored, and `require_verified` in the answer is `"true"` when it is on. When that file cannot be read or parsed it is `"unknown"`: only admins with a verified email are counted, so an admin who can sign in either way is kept, and a removed admin role is not brought back while the app still has an admin whose email is unverified (the `warning` then says that no admin can sign in if the setting is on, until one verifies their email). recovery.credentials.roles reports `primary_lowered`, `primary_reset` (accounts deleted since), `roles_kept_unknown` (accounts the live database never held, with a `note` saying so), `grants_kept`, `grants_removed`, `grants_expiry_shortened`, `usable_admins` and `require_verified`, plus `admins_kept`, `admins_kept_count` and `warning` when admin standing was kept; the `restore_credentials` audit row carries the same. When the live database is missing or unreadable, nothing says which roles or shares were taken away: they stay as in the snapshot and `skipped` says why (`live_unreadable` or `no_live_database`). The audit rows of role events recorded since the snapshot are copied too: api(at:"recovery.restore_audit_rows").

Record shares (`_benmore_permissions`, api(at:"permissions")) follow the same rule: a restore does not bring back a share that was revoked. A share in the snapshot survives only when the live database still holds a share of the same row to the same address and it has not expired there. It then gives only what both permissions give (`admin` covers every other; `edit` and `delete` each cover `view`; `edit` against `delete` leaves `view`) and takes the earlier of the two expiry times. A share revoked or expired since the snapshot is not in the restored database, a share made since the snapshot is not added, and a row hard-deleted since the snapshot comes back with the data but without its shares (share it again). A share the live database never held is kept: one on a row it never held (a whole-number id above every id that table holds or has handed out, or any id with no such row and no `_benmore_audit_log` row about it in the live database), or one to the address of an account it never held (the account test above). So a backup restored onto a recreated or new database keeps its shares, except a share on a row id the new database has already used that goes to an account id it has already used or to an address with no account. A live table that cannot be read counts as having held the row, so a share is never kept because of a failed read. recovery.credentials.record_grants reports `kept`, `removed`, `permission_lowered`, `expiry_shortened` and `kept_unknown` (shares the live database never held, with a `note`); the `restore_credentials` audit row carries the same.

Membership-table rows, and the roles an app keeps in them (`groups.role_field`, the tables `member-of:` and `ws_rooms:` read), are the app's own data and are restored with it: a member removed since the snapshot is a member again after a restore, with the tenant role the snapshot gives them. Remove them again after the restore, or restore to a point after the removal.
