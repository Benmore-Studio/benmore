GET /health?deep=1 is a readiness check for uptime monitors that fails when users would see a stall. It answers 503 {status:'unhealthy', ready:false, reason, version, instance_id, reload_generation} with reason `database_timeout` (a plain database read did not finish within 1 s: every connection busy, or the app stalled), `database_error` (that read failed), or `memory_pressure` (the app spent at least a fifth of the last 10 s stalled on memory reclaim), and otherwise 200 with ready:true plus the usual fields. It answers within 1.5 s and before any session lookup. The probe is never a transaction, so a writer holding the write lock does not fail it. Results are cached 5 s per app with one measurement in flight, so polling costs at most one probe per 5 s; a change logs `health: not ready: <reason> (...)` or `health: ready again`. Plain GET /health is separate (200 `healthy` with status, version, instance_id and reload_generation; restart verification, deploy, promote and the release tooling wait for exactly that), and its status never reflects the probe or memory. Admin sessions see checks.db_probe (status ok|slow|timeout|error, latency, checked_at) and, on Linux with cgroup v2, checks.memory (status ok|pressure, current_bytes, limit_bytes and limit_file from the lowest memory.high or memory.max on the cgroup's path, used_percent, pressure_some_avg10, pressure_full_avg10, go_memory_limit_bytes). memory.current near the limit is not a failure: it counts reclaimable page cache. Point external monitors at /health?deep=1; keep restart and deploy checks on /health.
